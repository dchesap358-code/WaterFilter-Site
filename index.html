Why build one?

5
Materials. That’s all.

Most of us never see what happens between a dirty glass and a clear one.

So we made it visible. You cut a bottle, stack gravel, sand and carbon, and pour. Muddy water goes in the top. Something much clearer drips out the bottom.

It costs almost nothing, takes an afternoon, and teaches more about water than any diagram could.

Four steps.

1

Prepare

Cut the bottle’s bottom off, flip it neck-down, and pierce a small hole in the cap so water can drip out.

2

Layer

Carbon first, then sand, small gravel and large gravel. Keep each layer flat and even.

3

Rinse

Flush with clean water until the carbon runs clear. Fresh carbon sheds black dust, so don’t skip this.

4

Filter

Pour dirty water slowly over the top, let gravity do the work, and catch it in a clean glass.

Little things that help.

01

Cloudy at first

The first run through fresh sand can look murky. Keep pouring until it clears.

02

Slow is better

The longer water spends in the sand and carbon, the better it works. A drip beats a gush.

03

Clean and refresh

Rinse gravel and sand when the flow slows. Carbon wears out, so swap it now and then.

Warning // Read this

Clearer is not the same as safe.

What it does

Removes particles, cloudiness and odour.

What it doesn’t

Reliably remove bacteria, viruses or dissolved chemicals.

So always

Boil filtered water for at least one minute before drinking.
